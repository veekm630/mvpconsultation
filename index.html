Answers.

For pharmacists

How long does it take to open a pharmacy?

Most projects run from six months to a year, driven mainly by your state board's licensing timeline and payer credentialing. Starting the applications in the right order is the single biggest way to shorten it — that's a core part of what we do.

Should I buy an existing pharmacy or start fresh?

Both paths work, and the right one depends on your market, capital, and goals. A purchase gets you script volume from day one; a fresh start avoids inherited problems and lets you build the pharmacy you actually want. We'll help you weigh the real numbers on both.

Can you help me add specialty or compounding services?

Yes — this is where we live. We operate a dermatology-focused specialty pharmacy ourselves and can guide you through specialty accreditation, compounding, and building clinical service lines that differentiate your store.

For entrepreneurs

Can I own a pharmacy if I'm not a pharmacist?

In most states, yes — you'll hire a pharmacist-in-charge to oversee practice. A small number of states restrict non-pharmacist ownership, so confirming your state's rules is one of the first things we do together.

What does it cost to start a pharmacy?

It varies widely with your location, buildout, and inventory strategy — which is exactly why we start every engagement with a realistic budget. Wholesaler deposits, initial inventory, and working capital for the credentialing period are the pieces most first-timers underestimate.
